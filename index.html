Mission 02 / Connect

From deep Earth to the surface

Build the journey

Match each numbered feature to its name.

Think beyond the picture

Earth’s layers: crust → mantle → outer core → inner core.

The crust and uppermost solid mantle form tectonic plates. The mantle is mostly solid rock that can flow very slowly; it is not a sea of liquid magma.

At some plate boundaries, conditions allow rock to melt. Magma can rise through cracks because it is less dense than surrounding rock.

Revision clue: why does NZ have volcanoes?

New Zealand lies across the Australian and Pacific plates. Beneath much of the North Island, the Pacific Plate subducts beneath the Australian Plate. Water released from the descending plate helps rock in the overlying mantle melt. Not every NZ volcano has this origin: Auckland’s volcanic field is an intraplate example.

Quick check: Explain aloud why “magma comes from Earth’s core” is incorrect.

Mission 03 / Make connections

Our volcanic neighbourhood

Read the three patterns, then test your connections.

01

Cone volcano

A volcanic mountain built by repeated eruptions. Layers of lava and fragmented volcanic material can build a steep cone.

Example: Ruapehu
02

Caldera volcano

A large depression formed when the ground collapses after magma is withdrawn from a reservoir during an eruption.

Example: Taupō
03

Volcanic field

An area with many separate vents and small volcanoes. Future eruptions may occur at a new location within the field.

Example: Auckland
Extension: where do shield volcanoes fit?

“Shield” describes a broad, gently sloping volcanic shape built mainly by fluid lava. “Volcanic field” describes an area containing multiple volcanoes. These terms describe different features, so they are not interchangeable.

Mission 04 / Distinguish

Same volcano. Different hazards.

Match each description to a hazard. Pay attention to what each flow contains.

Spot the misconception

“A town far from a volcano is always safe because lava cannot reach it.”

Compare with a model explanation

Wind can carry ash far from a volcano. Ash can affect breathing, contaminate water and disrupt transport, so a distant town can still be affected even if lava never reaches it.
